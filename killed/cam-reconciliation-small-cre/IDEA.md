# CAM reconciliation tool for small independent commercial property managers

## Concept
A tool for independent landlords/managers of small commercial (NNN or modified-gross) portfolios
that ingests lease terms and shared operating expenses, allocates Common Area Maintenance (CAM)
costs to tenants per their lease's pro-rata/cap terms, tracks CAM caps, and generates tenant-facing
annual reconciliation statements — sitting alongside the QuickBooks Online + spreadsheet combo this
segment already uses for day-to-day accounting.

## Target user
An independent commercial landlord/property manager with a small portfolio (under ~20 retail/
industrial/office units) running NNN or modified-gross leases, who uses QuickBooks + spreadsheets
for daily accounting but has no dedicated CAM reconciliation tooling.

## Problem
Per the sourcing forum thread, this segment's spreadsheet + QuickBooks workflow "works until CAM
reconciliation season, at which point it turns into a nightmare" — allocating shared expenses
across tenants per lease terms, applying caps, and producing tenant statements becomes an annual
crunch.

## Why now
No regulatory or platform trigger. Sourced via a general web/forum search (BiggerPockets +
Capterra), not one of this project's four named vendor-surface channels.

## Property case
- (a) plausible: shared operating expenses and lease terms change year to year.
- (b) plausible: tied to an annual/quarterly recurring reconciliation cycle, not a one-shot
  artifact.
- (c) plausible: a wrong CAM allocation either shorts the landlord or triggers a tenant dispute.

## Highest-priority risk flagged before Validate
Same forum thread that supplied the demand evidence also names two products already aimed at this
exact segment: STRATAFOLIO (general commercial-property/QuickBooks-integrated accounting, $160-230+
/mo) and, more decisively, **PigJet** — an AI-powered platform explicitly built for "portfolios
managing between 3 and 25 NNN properties" doing lease abstraction, CAM/NNN calculation, and
tenant-ready reconciliation statements. A same-thread reply also directly argues the diagnosis is
wrong ("the problem is almost never software. It's workflow"). See `VALIDATION.md`.
