**Pattern identified.** All three kills are single-purpose output generators: a value lookup (EOL
date), a filled form (security questionnaire answers), a drafted document (rent-increase notice
letter). In each case the entire deliverable is a static artifact that a free calculator, a free
adjacent product, or a generic LLM prompt already produces completely in one pass — so once the
first output is generated there is no reason left for the user to keep paying. This is what drove
low "reason to exist" and/or "willingness to pay" scores in all three, independent of genuine demand
(which was real in at least two of the three).

**Sharpened method.** Decided to select, in Phase 0, and check explicitly and early in Phase 1
(before spending the full research budget on all five scoring dimensions), for at least one of
three properties an idea can have that a one-shot free/DIY/LLM substitute cannot:
(a) value from data that changes on an ongoing basis and must be kept current, not a one-time
computation; (b) value from being embedded in a recurring workflow or persisted system state (a
running check, a scheduled alert, a kept record) rather than a single generated artifact; (c) a
cost of being wrong high enough (money owed, legal exposure, a filed document) that a free/DIY
answer isn't trusted even when one exists. Recorded the full analysis in `DECISIONS.md`
(2026-09-07 entry).
